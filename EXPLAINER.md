# SharkToothIsland.org: the one-sheet explainer

*For anyone new to the project, and for explaining it to a middle-school class or a parent. Updated Nov 9, 2026. This repo is public, so this sheet holds no private details, and `_config.yml` keeps it off the live site.*

## What this is, in one breath

A free website about Shark Tooth Island, a small island in the Cape Fear River near Wilmington where fossil shark teeth, millions of years old, wash up along the shore. The site helps people plan a visit, find teeth, and figure out what they found. It calls itself "the independent guide to the little fossil island," made by Mozy Outdoors.

## Who it's for

Fossil hunters, kayakers, families and anyone curious about ancient sharks. Mozy Outdoors appears only as the footer credit; island content never advertises Mozy.

## Where the information comes from

- **NOAA** (the US agency for oceans and weather): tide predictions from Orton Point on the Cape Fear River.
- **Open-Meteo:** a free weather forecast service, for wind and rain at the island.
- **County records:** the island's location, 34.1175 N, 77.93066 W, from the county parcel map.
- **Science papers:** the size calculator uses a collector's rule of thumb and points to a 2021 study (Perez and others) for how scientists estimate size. Sources are listed on each page.
- **Photos:** real photos only, by Rob, with drone photos by Jed Whitley.

## The ideas behind it

- **The island is new; the teeth are old.** The island is made of sand and mud dredged from the river's shipping channel. The teeth in that material are millions of years old, from the Miocene and Pliocene, long before people.
- **Low tide is hunting time.** Teeth turn up on the shoreline that low tide uncovers. Arriving about an hour before low tide gives you the most beach.
- **Sharks shed teeth all their lives,** and teeth are hard enough to fossilize, which is why teeth are found when the rest of the shark is gone.
- **Tooth size tells body size.** A bigger tooth means a bigger shark. For Megalodon, a rough rule is body length in feet is about tooth length in inches times 8.5.
- **Short, plain, sourced.** No hype, and no claims the sources don't back up.

## Questions to ask a class

- How can an island be younger than the fossils on it?
- Why would a shark leave behind teeth but not bones?
- If a Megalodon tooth is 5 inches long, about how long was the shark?

## Words to know

**Dredge spoil:** sand and mud dug out of a channel to keep it deep enough for ships, then piled somewhere else.
**Fossil:** the remains of a living thing turned to stone over a very long time.
**Megalodon:** a giant shark that died out millions of years ago.
**Miocene and Pliocene:** two stretches of Earth's history, roughly 23 million to 2.6 million years ago.
**Tide station:** a spot where NOAA measures and predicts the water level.
