Privacy.

The short version: we count visits so we know what's useful, we keep your email only if you give it to us, and we don't sell anything about you to anyone.

SharkToothIsland.org is a free field guide run by Mozy Outdoors. This page explains what the site collects, why, and which outside services are involved. Questions? Email Station@sharktoothisland.org.

Visit counts (Google Analytics)

We use Google Analytics to understand how people use the site: which pages get read, roughly where visitors are from (city or region, not your address), what kind of device and browser you're on, and how you found us. It also counts email signups and field guide requests. We use this to decide what to build next and to show local partners how many people the site reaches, as totals, never as a list of who visited.

Google Analytics sets cookies in your browser and receives your IP address to do this. We don't send it your name or email address. Google explains its side at How Google uses information from sites that use its services.

To opt out, block cookies in your browser, use a tracking blocker, or install Google's Analytics opt-out add-on. The site works the same either way.

The field guide and the email list

When you request the free field guide or sign up for Field Notes, we receive your email address and which page you used. Your address is stored with our email service, MailerLite, and we send a welcome email right away. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link. The guide downloads right away either way.

We use your email only for Shark Tooth Island news, good hunting tides and trip updates. We don't sell or rent it, and we don't share it with sponsors. Want off the list? Use the unsubscribe link in any email, or write to Station@sharktoothisland.org.

Tooth ID submissions

When you send a find through Tooth ID, we receive your photos, your email address, where on the island you found it, and any notes you add. Your browser shrinks each photo and removes its location and camera data before it leaves your device.

Submissions are stored in our Google account (Google Sheets and Google Drive) and arrive in our inbox by email, so we can look at your find and reply. We use your email to send you what you found and any follow-up about that find. We only add you to the email list if you leave the “also send me” box ticked, and that list works as described above.

We never post your photos or your find publicly without asking you first. If you say yes, the Station Finds gallery shows your photo, what it is, its size, the month you found it and the name you want credited. Never your email. To have a submission deleted, email Station@sharktoothisland.org.

Live tides and weather

The Hunt Planner and the live tide window load data straight from public sources while you're on the page, so your browser contacts these services directly and, like any website, they can see your IP address:

  • NOAA Tides & Currents (tide predictions)
  • Open-Meteo (weather forecasts)

We don't send them anything about you, and your plans stay in your browser.

Fonts, styling and hosting

Fonts and icons load from Google Fonts, and page styling loads from the Tailwind CSS CDN. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which keeps standard server logs (like IP address and the pages requested) to run and protect the site.

What we don't do

  • No ad networks or retargeting pixels.
  • No selling or renting of personal information.
  • No accounts, logins or payment details on this site.
  • Nothing aimed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their information.

Changes

If we add something new, like a sponsor's link tracking or a paid guide, we'll update this page and the date at the top.

Shark Tooth Island sits on a working tidal river. Paddling, boating, and fossil hunting carry real risk. This site is educational; it is not a guide service and does not guarantee conditions. It is an independent guide, not affiliated with the State of North Carolina, New Hanover County or the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. You are responsible for your own safety: check weather and tides, wear a PFD, and do not attempt without the skills and gear for moving water.
